Margin before volume

Before you buy another order, find out what the last one kept.

The number on the dashboard

Most growth reports lead with revenue. Revenue is easy to see, easy to celebrate and easy to grow, if you’re willing to pay for it. A store can double its orders and end the year with less cash than it started.

The question that matters is smaller: when a customer pays you, how much of that payment is still yours after you’ve paid for everything that got them there?

Contribution margin, in one line

Contribution margin is what’s left of an order after product cost, shipping and fulfillment, payment fees and the ad spend that earned it. It sits before rent, salaries and software, which is why it’s the number a growth team can actually move.

Per order, it’s simple: AOV minus variable costs minus CAC. If it’s positive on the first order, you can scale with confidence. If it’s negative, repeat purchases have to cover the gap, and you should know by how much and by when.

A worked example

Take a brand that sells at a $60 AOV. Product costs $18, shipping and fulfillment cost $8, and payment fees take 3%. Here’s what three different plans leave behind.

LineTodayVolume playMargin play
AOV$60.00$60.00$72.00
Product cost$18.00$18.00$21.60
Shipping and fulfillment$8.00$8.00$8.60
Payment fees$1.80$1.80$2.16
Contribution before ads$32.20$32.20$39.64
CAC$28.00$36.00$28.00
Left after acquisition$4.20-$3.80$11.64

*Illustrative. A fictional brand with made-up numbers.

The volume play spends 40% more to buy more orders. CAC rises as the audience widens, and each new order now loses $3.80 on day one. The margin play leaves traffic alone. It adds a bundle that lifts AOV to $72, and each order leaves $11.64.

The volume play can still win if repeat purchases are strong enough. That’s the point: you should know the number it needs to hit before you spend.

The rule we use: if you can’t say how much one order leaves after ads, don’t raise the budget yet.

Three places margin hides

  1. Shipping thresholds. A free-shipping line set years ago often sits right at your AOV, so it gives away shipping on orders that would have paid for it.
  2. Discount stacking. Welcome codes, sale prices and bundle discounts can pile up on a single order. Add them up on one cart before you run a promotion.
  3. Blended ROAS. One average hides the campaigns that lose money behind the ones that print it. Break it out by campaign and by first order.

What to do on Monday

Open your last 90 days of orders and work out contribution margin per order, on the first purchase only. Then split it by campaign. You’ll likely find one or two campaigns carrying the rest.

Move budget toward the campaigns that clear break-even, and run a single AOV test on your best-selling product. That’s the groundwork. The rest of growth goes better once you’ve done it.
